With the order book

Average $0.68

You work through four price levels. The real cost is about 10% higher, which can wipe out the strategy's profit.

Venues keep trades forever. They show the order book live and then throw it away.

Illustrative numbers: buying 50,000 shares costs 2,000 x $0.62 + 5,000 x $0.64 + 12,000 x $0.67 + 31,000 x $0.70 = $34,180, an average of $0.684, about 10% above $0.62. Prediction markets and Hyperliquid both work this way. Our lending history is different: raw on-chain events are stored, so it can be rebuilt later.

A lending rate rises as the cash runs out. A high rate can mean strong demand or a run.

1

Lenders deposit cash in a market. Borrowers take it out and pay interest.

2

The more of the cash is borrowed, the higher the rate goes. The protocol does this automatically to attract new lenders.

3

When almost all the cash is borrowed, the rate jumps, and lenders cannot withdraw until someone repays or deposits.

Healthy

Borrowers want the cash, rates rise, new lenders arrive, and everyone can still leave.

A run

Lenders are trying to leave and cannot. The high rate is a symptom of being stuck.

A ranking sorted by APY puts both cases at the top. It cannot tell them apart.

Aave and similar protocols set rates on a curve: the rate rises with utilization, the share of deposits that is borrowed, and climbs steeply near 100%.

When Aave's WETH yield rose fourfold, lenders were trapped.

A vault can only pay you back from cash that nobody has borrowed.

1

You deposit USDC in a vault.

2

A curator lends your USDC out across several lending markets.

3

Borrowers in those markets take most of the cash.

4

When you withdraw, the vault can only take the cash in those markets that is not lent out.

When several vaults lend into the same market, they share the same unborrowed cash. Whoever withdraws first gets paid.

Morpho vaults allocate deposits across lending markets chosen by a curator. A withdrawal is paid from the unborrowed cash in those markets. Curators and the public allocator can move funds between markets, which this simple picture leaves out.
