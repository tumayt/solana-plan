Architecture
01

Pooled liquidity model

Users deposit into shared pools and earn interest. Borrowers draw from those same pools against collateral. Each position is overcollateralized.

Liquidity is pooled across all borrowers of a given asset regardless of collateral type.

Reserve configurations go through governance, not permissionless deployment.

Governance-gated Pooled risk Flash loans
Pools (per asset)
Each token is a reserve with its own LTV, liquidation threshold, caps. Suppliers receive aTokens that accrue interest in real-time.

Unlike Morpho and Euler, there are no isolated markets per collateral pair.
Risk parameters
02

Health factor is aggregated across the entire account. A drop in one collateral affects every borrow in the position.

Isolation mode
Contain the blast radius
Riskier assets get listed with hard constraints: only approved stablecoins can be borrowed, a debt ceiling caps total exposure, and the isolated asset must be the sole collateral.
E-mode
Higher LTV for correlated assets
When collateral and borrow are highly correlated (USDC/USDT, ETH/stETH), governance encodes that relationship explicitly. Users get higher LTVs and tighter liquidation thresholds.
Siloed borrowing
Restrict on the borrow side
Borrowing a siloed asset means that is the only thing the user can borrow in that position. Targets assets with low liquidity or manipulatable oracles. No debt ceiling, no collateral restriction.
Governance lever: borrowing power of any reserve can be reduced to 0% for new borrows without affecting existing positions.

Liquidators cannot always clear the full position at once. When health factor is between 0.95 and 1, only up to 50% of debt can be repaid. Below 0.95, the full position is available.

Oracles & governance
04

Oracles

Oracle-agnostic at the reserve level. The community selects price feeds through governance, unlike Morpho where market creators choose or Euler where vault governors configure.

Chainlink
Standard feeds
Aggregates from multiple sources, updates on time or price deviation. Reliable but can lag during extreme volatility.
CAPO
Correlated price oracle
Designed for assets tracking another closely, such as LSTs. Mirrors underlying price feed. Reduces asynchronicity risk in E-mode categories.

Governance roles

Access control list separates powers across entities. These roles operate at the protocol level, not per market. Contrast with Morpho and Euler where governance is vault-scoped.

Pool admin
Full control. Can activate, deactivate, pause, and drop reserves entirely. Same as Owner in Morpho vaults.
Risk admin
Day-to-day parameter changes: LTV, liquidation threshold, bonus, supply/borrow caps, E-mode categories. Maps to Curator in Morpho.
Asset listing admin
Adds new assets and updates oracle sources. Controls which reserves exist and which feeds they use.
Emergency admin
Pause and unpause the pool or individual reserves. Same concept as Guardian in Morpho.
Interest rate model & fees
05

Two-slope model

Each reserve has an optimal usage ratio, the point where supply and demand are considered balanced. Below it rates rise gradually. Above it they spike sharply to attract liquidity back.

Below optimal utilization
Rates increase along a shallow slope, encouraging borrowing without being punishing. Suppliers earn proportionally to how much of the pool is being used.
Above optimal utilization
Rates spike sharply along a second steeper slope. The jump is intentional, it incentivizes repayments and attracts new liquidity back into the pool fast.
Governance-controlled slopes
Unlike Morpho's adaptive curve, slopes are fixed parameters. When market conditions shift, the risk admin manually updates the interest rate strategy per reserve. More control, less autonomy.

Fee structure

Aave takes cuts at the reserve level before interest reaches suppliers. Morpho takes a performance fee at the vault layer. Same economic outcome, different place in the stack.

Reserve factor
Percentage of borrower interest redirected to the DAO treasury. Configured per reserve by governance. Different assets carry different rates.
Liquidation protocol fee
A share of the liquidation bonus goes to the treasury instead of entirely to the liquidator. Set per reserve, so riskier assets tend to carry higher bonuses overall.
Flash loan fee
Split between aToken holders and the treasury. No collateral required, but the full amount plus fee must be returned within the same transaction.

Key takeaways
08

What to remember

01

Pooled model trades isolation for capital efficiency

Unlike Morpho where risk is contained per market, Aave pools liquidity across all borrowers. A problem in one collateral affects every supplier of that asset.

02

Governance is the risk manager

Reserve parameters, oracle selection, and asset listing all go through governance. Slower than Morpho or Euler, but results in more coordinated risk management across the protocol.

03

Umbrella is a structural fix, not a patch

Staking aTokens directly to cover asset-specific deficits removes the old inefficiency of selling AAVE to cover USDC losses. Insurance logic borrowed from traditional finance, applied on-chain.

04

V4 prices risk per borrower, not per pool

The user risk premium internalizes an externality V3 ignores. In V3 a risky borrower implicitly raised rates for everyone. In V4 they pay for their own collateral choices.

Two mechanics worth understanding

aToken balance accrual
How it works

aTokens accrue interest without any new tokens being minted. The balance grows every second, so the amount shown at withdrawal is always higher than what was deposited. No interaction needed.

Aave vs Morpho: receipt token mechanics
Aave: aTokens

Issued at 1:1 with the underlying. Balance grows in nominal value as the index increases. Interest is baked into every balanceOf call.

Morpho: vault shares

Issued at an exchange rate reflecting total vault assets. Share count stays fixed between interactions. Value accrues through the rising exchange rate, not a rebasing balance.

Same economic outcome. Different token mechanics.

Morpho deposit path vs Aave deposit path
Morpho: User → Router → Vault → Morpho main contract
Aave: User → Pool Proxy → aToken contract
