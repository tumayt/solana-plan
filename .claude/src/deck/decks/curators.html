DeFi lending
curators

What a curator is, and why the role exists
01

The role

A curator is a non-custodial risk manager. It runs an ERC-4626 vault on top of a permissionless lending protocol like Morpho or Euler. The curator picks which markets the vault enters, sets supply caps, and rebalances the allocations. Depositors hand over the underlying asset and receive vault shares. The share price accrues yield because borrowers pay interest in the underlying markets.

Why it exists

DeFi liquidity is fragmented across hundreds of isolated markets. Choosing which markets to enter, at what size, and when to rotate is a full-time job. A curator is the entity with the expertise and the track record to do it.

Two contrasts that define the role
Against TradFi
A TradFi fund manager takes custody of investor capital. A curator never does. Everything is onchain and auditable.
Against the protocol
The curator manages risk for the deposited capital. The protocol is the rails the curator works on.

The role was formalized by Morpho in early 2024.

Euler Earn picked up the same model soon after.

In mid-2025 Kamino brought the concept to Solana.

Roles inside a vault
02

A vault generally splits control across four roles. The curator and allocator are usually the same entity.

01 · Admin
Owner
Vault admin, typically a multisig. Assigns roles, sets fees, manages timelocks.
02 · Risk
Curator
Selects strategies and sets supply caps for each market.
03 · Ops
Allocator
Executes rebalances within the curator's policy.
04 · Veto
Guardian
Holds veto power over pending critical actions.
Economics
03

How curators earn

The TradFi pair carries over. A performance fee runs 10 to 15 percent of yield generated, though a few large vaults run at 0 to attract liquidity. With Morpho V2 some curators add a small management fee on AUM.

The incentive misalignment

Curators earn fees on AUM and yield, but depositors bear 100 percent of the tail risk. A curator can boost revenue by allocating to riskier markets, knowing it will not personally suffer if those bets fail. Reputation and transparency are the only mitigations the model offers.

How long the role has existed
05

The curator role as a distinct primitive is roughly two years old. The firms doing the curating are not. Most ran as risk advisory shops first.

Gauntlet advised Aave, Compound, and MakerDAO on risk parameters since 2018. Steakhouse built MakerDAO's RWA accounting standards from 2020. When the model formalized, these firms migrated to monetize their expertise through performance fees rather than fixed retainers.

Curator TVL grew from roughly $300M in early 2025 to a peak above $10B in late 2025, then settled near $5.9B after the Stream Finance unwind.

How they curate · the workflow
06

The day-to-day work is largely the same across curators. The implementation differs, the shape does not.

01
Risk assessment
Screen candidate markets on liquidity, oracle quality, contract maturity, governance, and collateral correlation.
02
Caps
Per-market caps on a whitelist. Increases are timelocked. Decreases are immediate.
03
Queues
Supply queue takes new deposits. Withdraw queue serves redemptions. The allocator rebalances between them.
04
Monitoring
Alerts on utilization spikes, oracle staleness, depegs, governance proposals, and role changes.

Cap increases give depositors advance warning of risk-increasing changes. Cap decreases stay immediate so curators can react to deteriorating conditions without delay.

The risk surface · inherent market risks
08

Curator risk splits into two kinds. The first are inherent: they come from the lending markets themselves, and every vault carries them no matter how well the curator operates.

Liquidity risk
When utilization runs hot, most supplied assets are borrowed out. Immediate withdrawal capacity shrinks. Better cap management reduces this, but it never disappears entirely.
Rate volatility
When borrow demand softens or utilization normalizes, yield compresses. No curator controls borrower demand, so rate compression is always possible regardless of allocation quality.
Bad debt risk
When borrower positions are not liquidated in time during sharp moves, uncovered shortfalls socialize across all lenders in that market. Fast liquidator action reduces the window, but cannot guarantee it closes to zero.
The risk surface · curation risks
09

The second kind comes from the choices a curator makes. These vary vault to vault, and a depositor can verify all of them onchain without taking anything on trust.

Governance and multisig setup Five vault roles (owner, curator, allocator, guardian, sentinel) are typically controlled by multisigs. Signers, thresholds, and how roles split across separate multisigs all matter. A single multisig holding every role is a single point of failure. Checkable onchain.
Collateral quality The assets a vault lends against determine most of its tail risk. The collateral set is the clearest single read on how aggressive a mandate really is, more direct than any published framework.
Oracle configuration Every market depends on a price feed. Hardcoded prices, single-source feeds, and feeds that thin liquidity can manipulate are failure modes that sit underneath an otherwise healthy-looking vault.
Market concentration A vault spread across many markets absorbs a single-market failure without systemic loss. A concentrated one does not. Per-market supply caps are the standard mitigation: inspect them directly.

Steakhouse Financial · ~$1.82B
10

Steakhouse runs its product lines through onchain vaults on Morpho and Kamino rather than spreading across many platforms. Three product lines.

Prime
Institutional grade
Collateral restricted to blue-chip assets, crypto-native and tokenized TradFi. Aragon DAO as guardian.
High Yield
Broader collateral
Accepts exotic assets. Fees around 10 percent because the vault needs more frequent reallocation.
Turbo
Highest yields
Built on Morpho V2 with a custom adapter registry. Leveraged looping and carry strategies.

Four strategy primitives compose every position: Repo, overcollateralized lending with instant liquidity. Term, liquidity locked for a defined period. Carry, borrow low and lend high to earn the spread. Looping, carry on correlated pairs like ETH and stETH.

Steakhouse · three-system stack
11

Three systems gate every dollar in a Steakhouse vault. Each one answers a different question.

Multi-layer rating
Decides which collateral can be lent against at all.
Allocation process
Decides which eligible markets actually get capital.
Runtime stack
Decides how the vault reacts when conditions change.
Steakhouse · multi-layer risk rating
12

The rating produces a single AA-to-C score that decides whether a market qualifies and which product range it lands in. The central design choice is that market parameters can partly compensate for a riskier asset. A risky asset wrapped in tight LLTV, a careful oracle, and a well-correlated pair can still be acceptable. A safe asset exposed through aggressive configuration is not.

Every criterion uses the same six-step AA-to-C scale. Aggregation is worst-of, because the weakest pillar sets a layer's strength.

Layer 1 · Asset
Quality of the collateral itself. Three pillars: issuer, which carries the structural weight, credit risk, and operational risk.
Layer 2 · Platform
Resilience of the protocol that transforms one asset into another.
Layer 3 · Market
Risk parameters of the lending venue, could be oracle, liquidity.

Allocation process

Once a market clears the rating gate, the allocation process decides if it gets capital. At least two risk-team members review the asset and market configuration across six vectors.

Credit · Counterparty · Asset liquidity · Oracle · Smart contract · Liquidity trap

The goal is to keep depositors out of markets the rating would technically allow but where idiosyncratic risk is too high.

Steakhouse · runtime stack
14

The runtime stack acts before incidents become losses. It splits into a signal layer that watches and a response layer that acts.

Signal layer, four categories
Price
Oracle-DEX divergences and depegs on correlated assets.
Onchain activity
Borrowing spikes and high-frequency deposit-withdrawal patterns.
Governance
Issuer-side parameter changes that move economic state.
Issuer quality
Slower signals like missed roadmap milestones or proof-of-reserves changes.
Response layer, two paths
Reallocation engine
The slow path. Continuously moves liquidity between markets to track current risk.
Firefighter
The fast path. Withdraws liquidity from an affected market to force divestment.

DEX liquidity
The maximum that can be swapped without excessive slippage. Lets liquidators offload bad debt.

Historical price volatility on the collateral drives the LLTV ratio.

How manipulable the oracle is.

Maximizes risk-adjusted yield while targeting minimal insolvency risk even under extreme conditions.

Profile 3 of 4
Sentora · ~$1.43B
17

Sentora scaled through institutional partnerships, having started as IntoTheBlock. It curates the vaults behind Kraken DeFi Earn. Where Gauntlet runs five lines under one framework, Sentora runs two structurally different vault architectures.

Direct vaults

Deposits route straight into underlying lending protocols. Yield comes from borrower demand in the routed markets. This is the standard curator model.

Smart vaults

Instead of routing into a single protocol, the vault constructs structured positions across multiple venues, involving collateralization, borrowing, and redeployment.

The Stablecoin Smart Vault lets depositors enter with PYUSD, RLUSD, USDT, or USDC, against the single-token entry of standard vaults. It runs a recursive loop: deposit into Maple, borrow on Morpho, return to Maple. BTC and ETH Smart Vaults launched alongside it.

Sentora also runs a public DeFi risk dashboard. Its distinctive indicators track recursive lending: a health-factor distribution and a supply-share read on whether deposits are sticky.

Profile 4 of 4
KPK · ~$177M
18

KPK frames its model around agents managing vault funds. Agents rather than analysts handle most of the day-to-day work. Two named agents run the automation, both deterministic programs executing whitelisted functions.

Rebalancing agent

Reallocates across approved markets with a rules-based approach that is tier and cap aware, subject to liquidity and safety checks.

Exit agent

Responds to risk alerts like oracle staleness or price divergence within seconds, cutting exposure and raising withdrawable liquidity.

Both follow the same loop. A Hypernative alert triggers them, they fetch market data, estimate APYs and utilization across feasible allocations, and calculate the optimal allocation under predefined risk boundaries. Agents cannot introduce new strategies or expand exposure beyond approved limits.

Security model

The worst case of an automation key compromise is reallocation between pre-approved markets within existing caps. Funds cannot leave to an external address.

What the Stream Finance episode revealed
20

November 2025 is the only real curator stress test so far. A Balancer V2 stable-pool exploit drained roughly $130M and triggered cascading withdrawals. Stream Finance's xUSD lost its peg, and curator vaults with xUSD exposure absorbed the losses.
